What Computex 2026 Means For Your Next Gaming Rig (And Your Old One)

NVIDIA's SoC aims for new portable power, Intel shrinks down its Arc line, and AMD delivers a new CPU while bringing back an old favorite.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Aug 01, 2026 · 10:14 UTC 3 MIN

Computex 2026 has concluded, and it delivered a fascinating blend of forward-looking power plays and warm nods to hardware's enduring legacy. The announcements from NVIDIA, Intel, and AMD paint a clear picture: high-end gaming is looking for new form factors, but the roots of PC building remain strong.

NVIDIA's CEO Jensen Huang took the stage to announce the RTX Spark System-on-a-Chip (SoC), a bold stride into portable supercomputing. This new chip leverages an Arm-based N1X "superchip" that packs a serious punch. We're talking up to 20 Grace CPU cores alongside a staggering 6188 RTX Blackwell GPU cores. For context, that GPU core count matches what you'd find in an RTX 5070 desktop GPU, signaling a potent future for compact gaming devices.

Not to be outdone, Intel also revealed its intentions for the handheld market, introducing two new chips designed specifically for portable gaming: the Intel Arc G3 and Intel Arc G3 Extreme. These chips come equipped with "up to Intel Arc B390 graphics," featuring a configuration of two P-cores, eight E-cores, and four LPE cores. It's a clear signal that Intel is deepening its commitment to the growing handheld PC gaming segment.

Meanwhile, AMD approached the show with a dual-pronged CPU strategy that catered to both the bleeding edge and cherished tradition. They launched the Ryzen 7 7700X3D, a new AM5-based CPU available for $329. Simultaneously, AMD reached back into its history, re-releasing the beloved Ryzen 7 5800X3D for AM4 to celebrate the platform's 10-year anniversary. This move offers compelling choices whether you're building a brand-new system or looking to breathe new life into an older, but still capable, rig.

Collectively, these announcements offer a compelling look at where PC gaming hardware is headed. NVIDIA and Intel are clearly focused on miniaturizing powerful components to unlock new, more accessible form factors for high-performance gaming. At the same time, AMD's strategy shows a strong appreciation for the existing PC builder ecosystem, ensuring that both cutting-edge enthusiasts and those holding onto a beloved platform have exciting options. It’s a future where your next gaming rig might be smaller than ever, or a revitalized version of a trusted friend.
