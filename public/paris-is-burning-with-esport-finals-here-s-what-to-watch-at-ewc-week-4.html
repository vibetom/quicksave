Paris is Burning (With Esport Finals): Here's What to Watch at EWC Week 4

The Esports World Cup 2026 in Paris is heating up with major championship finals for Overwatch, Call of Duty: Warzone, and PUBG, all vying for slices of a record-breaking $75 million prize pool.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Jul 25, 2026 · 10:06 UTC 3 MIN

Paris has become the epicenter of competitive gaming this summer, as the Esports World Cup 2026 unfolds at the Paris Expo Porte de Versailles. The month-long spectacle, which began earlier in the summer, continues to draw global eyes and will run through August 23, delivering a constant stream of high-stakes esports action.

This fourth week of the EWC is particularly intense, as it brings several major championship finals to the forefront. Among them is the Overwatch Champions Series 2026 Midseason Championship, running from July 29 to August 2. Teams will battle it out for a substantial $1,000,000 prize pool, with the top squad taking home a hefty $400,000. After a year of renewed energy for Overwatch esports, strong viewership is expected for this event.

Simultaneously, the Call of Duty: Warzone Resurgence Series 2026 Championship is set to deliver its own brand of adrenaline-fueled competition. From July 29 to August 1, teams will vie for a share of another $1,000,000 prize pool, with the champions earning $250,000. It's a testament to the diverse landscape of competitive gaming that two such prominent titles are sharing the spotlight.

While Week 4 focuses on these new championships, the intensity of the EWC has been building. The PUBG: Battlegrounds Grand Finals, for instance, recently concluded on Sunday, July 26. In that event, 16 teams competed across 18 matches over three days for a share of a $2 million prize pool.

These championship events are not just isolated tournaments; they are crucial components of the larger Esports World Cup 2026, which boasts an unprecedented overall prize pool of $75 million. This staggering figure underscores the rapidly growing financial investment and increasing professionalization within the esports ecosystem.

The reach of the EWC extends far beyond the Parisian venue. The entire event is broadcast live to 160 countries, facilitated by over 100 broadcast and OTT partners, streaming platforms, and various digital services. With more than 7,000 hours of live programming on offer, the Esports World Cup ensures that fans worldwide can easily access and engage with the pinnacle of competitive gaming.
