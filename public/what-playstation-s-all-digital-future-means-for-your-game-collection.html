What PlayStation's All-Digital Future Means For Your Game Collection

Sony is officially phasing out physical game discs for new releases by January 2028. Here's what that means for how you'll buy and own games going forward.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Aug 01, 2026 · 12:32 UTC 3 MIN

Sony Interactive Entertainment has announced a major shift for the PlayStation ecosystem: physical game disc production for all new titles will officially cease starting in January 2028. This move means that after that date, every new game hitting PlayStation consoles will be available exclusively in digital formats, through the PlayStation Store or other retailers.

This isn't a sudden, quiet update; it's a confirmed directive from the company. For players, this marks a significant turning point, reshaping how collections will grow and how new experiences are accessed in the years to come.

It's important to note what this change does not impact. Any game currently available on disc, or any title scheduled for a physical release before January 2028, will remain unaffected. Your existing library of physical games will continue to function as always. The policy applies strictly to new games launching from the beginning of 2028 onward.

Sony cites a clear rationale for this transition: evolving consumer preferences. The company states that the broader entertainment landscape is steadily migrating away from physical media and towards digital consumption. According to Sony, this shift allows them to better align with the primary way most of their community already prefers to access and play games.

Looking ahead, Sony intends to keep prioritizing resources towards innovating game access, ensuring that new titles remain broadly available. While discs are out, the company says players will still have choices for purchasing new games, whether directly from the PlayStation Store or from other digital retailers.

Despite some expressed concerns from parts of the player base, Sony appears committed to this digital-only future for new releases. The company is not reversing its decision, indicating a firm course has been set for how PlayStation gamers will engage with their hobbies from 2028 on.
