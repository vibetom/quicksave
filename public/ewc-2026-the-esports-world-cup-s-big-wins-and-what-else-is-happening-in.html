EWC 2026: The Esports World Cup's Big Wins (And What Else is Happening in Esports)

From Overwatch and Street Fighter battles to new champions crowned in PUBG, FC Pro, and Teamfight Tactics, the EWC is delivering on its promise, even as other major esports events grapple with their own challenges.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Jul 29, 2026 · 16:00 UTC 4 MIN

The Esports World Cup (EWC) 2026 has entered its fourth week, showcasing a vibrant array of high-stakes competition across a diverse selection of titles. The event remains a key destination for premier esports, highlighting both ongoing contests and recently crowned champions.

Spectators are currently tuned into the OWCS Midseason Championship, running from July 29 to August 2. This event gathers sixteen of the world's top Overwatch teams, each aiming for a significant portion of the $1,000,000 prize pool, along with critical Club Championship points. The intensity is just as high in the Street Fighter 6 arena, where 32 of the globe's finest players are clashing for their share of another $1,000,000 in prizes. Adding to this rich tapestry, Mobile Legends: Bang Bang (MLBB) continues its tournament play this week, solidifying the mobile gaming presence within the EWC's extensive schedule.

The past week at the EWC 2026 has already delivered several unforgettable moments. Virtus.pro secured the PUBG: BATTLEGROUNDS trophy, besting 16 rival teams through an intense three-day Finals. In the FC Pro World Championship, Razvan “RvPLegend” Puiu clinched the title, overcoming Levy “levyfinn” Rieck with a 6-5 victory in a thrilling Grand Final to claim a $250,000 first prize. Meanwhile, Team Vision delivered a decisive 3-0 sweep against Aurora Gaming, earning the Teamfight Tactics trophy, $150,000, and 1,000 Club Championship points.

While the EWC 2026 firmly establishes itself as a leading multi-title event, the wider esports landscape is navigating its own distinct hurdles. Within the League of Legends community, considerable frustration is brewing over the resale costs of World Championship tickets, with some prices reportedly climbing past $3,000. While this issue captures significant attention, it has also been reported that Riot Games might not bear sole responsibility for the inflated secondary market. Concurrently, the LEC (League of Legends EMEA Championship) Summer Split 2026 is actively unfolding in Paris, France.

The Esports World Cup 2026 consistently delivers on its promise of compelling competition and substantial prize pools, presenting a dynamic range of games and celebrating new champions. This steady performance underscores its increasing influence, even as other major events navigate their specific challenges.
