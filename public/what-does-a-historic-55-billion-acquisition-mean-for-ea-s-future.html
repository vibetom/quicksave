What Does a Historic $55 Billion Acquisition Mean for EA's Future?

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund has finalized its takeover of Electronic Arts, taking the publisher private and signaling major shifts for the company, its employees, and its games.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Aug 05, 2026 · 16:14 UTC 2 MIN

Electronic Arts has been acquired by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) for a reported $55 billion. The deal, finalized today, August 5, 2026, takes the major publisher private, delisting it from the stock market and marking what is believed to be the largest leveraged buyout in history, according to The National News.

This significant move aligns with Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030, an initiative to diversify the nation's economy beyond its reliance on oil. Gaming and esports have been designated as "strategic sectors" for the PIF's investment efforts in achieving this vision, as reported by The National News.

The immediate aftermath of such a monumental shift often brings change, and Electronic Arts is no exception. Mass layoffs are reportedly expected within the company following the acquisition, according to GameSpot. This introduces a period of significant uncertainty for many within EA's workforce.

Alongside the ownership transition, EA CEO Andrew Wilson has already announced several senior leadership appointments. Among these changes, Cam Weber has been named chief studios officer and David Tinson has been promoted to chief operating officer, as confirmed by GamesIndustry.biz. These swift internal restructurings signal a new operational chapter for the publisher under its private ownership.

The investor consortium behind this acquisition includes Affinity Partners, an entity led by Jared Kushner, adding another dimension to the new management structure overseeing Electronic Arts. While the long-term implications for EA's development strategy and its beloved franchises remain to unfold, the immediate landscape for the company, its employees, and its strategic direction has clearly begun to shift under this historic new ownership.
