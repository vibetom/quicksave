Sweeney Strikes Again: Why Epic's CEO Can't Stop Talking About Steam's Cut

Tim Sweeney is once more beating the drum for an open PC platform, reigniting the age-old debate about developer payouts and what it means for the future of your games.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Jul 29, 2026 · 21:25 UTC 2 MIN

Tim Sweeney, the outspoken CEO of Epic Games, has once again voiced his long-standing critique of Steam's 30% revenue share, championing an open, competitive marketplace as the essential path forward for PC gaming. It's a familiar tune, but one that continues to resonate through the industry.

Sweeney's core argument, as reported by PC Gamer, centers on the belief that a more open platform would grant developers greater flexibility and could ultimately lead to more competitive pricing for players. The idea is simple: a healthier share for creators translates into more resources for innovation and, potentially, more attractive deals for consumers.

This isn't merely a philosophical stance for Sweeney. It's deeply embedded in Epic Games' business model. The Epic Games Store famously offers developers an 88/12 revenue split, meaning a substantial 88% of revenue goes directly to the game creators, with Epic taking just 12%. This model stands in stark contrast to the traditional 70/30 split common across many digital storefronts, including Valve's Steam.

While these recent remarks reinforce Sweeney's consistent and deeply held ideological position, they don't signal any immediate new initiatives or changes coming from Epic Games itself. Instead, it's a reaffirmation of the company's foundational approach to digital distribution and a continued push for what it sees as a more equitable ecosystem.

For years, Epic has positioned itself as an ally for developers, using its store's revenue split as a key differentiator. Sweeney's continued advocacy ensures that the conversation around platform fees and developer compensation remains at the forefront, impacting not just the bottom line for studios but potentially the very landscape of PC gaming for everyone who plays.
