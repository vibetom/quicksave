Counter-Strike 2 Grand Final Moves to Massive Accor Arena for EWC 2026

High demand for tickets prompted the Esports Foundation to relocate the Counter-Strike 2 championship to a significantly larger venue in Paris, hinting at a huge moment for the EWC.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Jul 28, 2026 · 16:17 UTC 3 MIN

The Esports World Cup (EWC) 2026 is already making waves, with its Counter-Strike 2 Grand Final officially moving to Paris' iconic Accor Arena. The Esports Foundation announced this significant venue upgrade, signaling a clear response to overwhelming fan enthusiasm.

This relocation comes after tickets for the original venue, the stc Arena at Paris Expo Porte de Versailles, sold out with impressive speed. It's a testament to the passionate Counter-Strike community and their hunger for live esports action.

Moving to the Accor Arena dramatically expands the event's footprint. The new venue boasts a capacity of 15,000 to 20,300 fans, a substantial leap from the approximately 5,000 seats available at the prior location. This means thousands more fans will get to witness the peak of Counter-Strike 2 competition, amplifying the energy and spectacle of the Grand Final.

The Accor Arena is no stranger to high-stakes Counter-Strike. It famously hosted the BLAST Paris 2023 Major, which was the final Major for Counter-Strike: Global Offensive. This familiarity ensures that the venue is well-equipped to deliver a top-tier experience for players and spectators alike.

The Counter-Strike 2 Championship Finals at EWC26 are set for August 23. Beyond the individual game championship, the Accor Arena will also be the stage for the awarding of the EWC 2026 Club Championship, bringing the entire seven-week esports extravaganza to a dramatic conclusion. This dual role elevates the Grand Final into an even more central event for the entire Esports World Cup.

This move speaks volumes about the burgeoning mainstream appeal of Counter-Strike 2 esports and the overall ambition of the Esports World Cup. It's a powerful signal that the competitive scene is not just thriving, but actively expanding its reach and impact.
