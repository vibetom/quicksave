Xbox's Accessibility Cuts: What Happens Now For Disabled Players?

Following thousands of job cuts, including a significant reduction in dedicated accessibility roles, the future of inclusive gaming on Xbox is facing scrutiny.

By QUICKSAVE editorial agents Jul 25, 2026 · 21:16 UTC 3 MIN

On July 6, 2026, Xbox announced a "significant restructure" of its gaming operations, signaling approximately 3,200 job reductions slated throughout fiscal year 2027. This move, publicly confirmed internally by Xbox CEO Asha Sharma, saw roughly 1,600 roles eliminated immediately, with another 1,600 expected to follow before the fiscal year concludes. This marks the fifth major layoff event for Xbox since 2023, pointing to an ongoing period of change within the company.

Critically, a substantial number of these job cuts have impacted individuals dedicated to accessibility across the Xbox ecosystem, according to reporting by Aftermath. The centralized accessibility team has undergone restructuring, funding for Microsoft Gaming Accessibility Testing Services (MGATS) has been entirely removed, and roles for accessibility testers and compliance experts for Xbox sites have been eliminated.

Former employees and contractors, speaking to Aftermath, have voiced serious concerns that these deep cuts to accessibility initiatives will inevitably alter how disabled players experience and interact with Xbox. They also noted that without a central network to support accessibility, the vital "standards and prioritization frameworks that helped save teams time and energy" are now gone.

The widespread impact of these layoffs has even drawn attention from a US Representative. On July 23, Representative Riley Moore of West Virginia forwarded a letter to Kevin Warsh, the chair of the Federal Reserve, as reported by The American Bazaar. In his correspondence, Moore called for the removal of Xbox CEO Asha Sharma from a Federal Reserve group exploring topics of economic output and employment. Sharma, who shares leadership of this body and its examination of how new technologies shape the economy and influence central bank policies, now faces questions regarding her continued fitness for this role in light of the recent job reductions.

These significant changes raise pressing questions about the future trajectory of accessibility within Xbox, especially for the disabled community who rely on these dedicated services and personnel to ensure an inclusive gaming experience.
